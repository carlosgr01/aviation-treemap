Air Passenger Markets in 2024

A pictogram-based comparison of six major national passenger markets. Every full airplane symbol represents 50 million passengers, turning large values into countable visual units.

Author: Carlos Gracia Ruiz  ·  Programme: Master’s Degree in Data Science
Course: Data Visualization · PEC 2

Isotype-style pictogram chart

Each repeated airplane represents 50 million passengers. The exact total is printed alongside each country; the faded airplane at the end represents any remaining fraction of 50 million.

✈ = 50 million passengers ✈ = remainder, scaled to the same unit

Unit chart: compare the counts

This second view emphasizes the number of countable units rather than using a continuous bar. Each square is one 50-million-passenger unit; the final partial unit is shown as a fraction.

What stands out?
The United States and China are substantially larger passenger markets than the other four countries in this selected group. Spain, India and Japan are relatively close to one another in the reported figures.
Interpret with care
These figures represent passengers arriving or departing in each country, not passengers carried by individual airlines. They are absolute totals, so they do not measure population-adjusted travel, airline reliability or the number of flights.
Source: International Air Transport Association (IATA), “IATA Launches 2024 World Air Transport Statistics Report”, 4 August 2025, section “Top Passenger Markets in 2024”.
